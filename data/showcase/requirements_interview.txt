Requirements Interview Practice
Fictional study material; not an official university document.

A mock interview asks what a reader needs after finding a relevant document. Participants request a short source preview, a complete reading view, and an original download. The analyst separates those usability needs from proposals for new matching algorithms. A request for a clearer interface does not automatically justify semantic retrieval, metadata filters, or a large deployment architecture.

The software team turns each request into an observable acceptance criterion. An indexed filename opens its corresponding text. A download preserves bytes. A keyword limit preserves the ranking prefix. An invalid Boolean field produces understandable feedback before submission. The group checks that each criterion can be verified without inventing hidden product capabilities.

One Java developer explains the existing constraints: ASCII-focused tokens, fixed fictional collections, and a server that indexes at startup. The interview records uncertainties about real document rights and future accounts as open decisions. Participants avoid invented quotations or approvals. A final exercise rewrites vague phrases such as secure and fast into bounded statements backed by specific tests, while acknowledging that prototype evidence is not a production guarantee.
